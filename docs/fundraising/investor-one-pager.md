# AI Action Browser — Investor One-Pager

Status: pre-launch prototype. No live search, payment, or merchant execution is currently connected.

## Thesis

AI will change the browser from a page-navigation tool into a consumer action layer.

People should be able to describe what they want, receive a sourced comparison, and prepare the next step without learning prompt engineering or surrendering final control.

## Product

AI Action Browser is a free consumer product that:

1. understands a natural-language goal;
2. searches and organizes relevant evidence;
3. compares options and explains trade-offs;
4. keeps recommendations independent from commercial bids;
5. prepares an action;
6. requires explicit confirmation before important external actions.

The first validation market is the United States. The first narrow use case is laptop shopping.

## Why now

- consumer AI has created demand for direct answers and task completion;
- model and inference markets are becoming competitive;
- structured commerce and affiliate infrastructure can fund free consumer access;
- browsers remain the distribution and permission layer for web actions;
- current AI assistants often mix chat, search, and action without a sufficiently clear trust boundary.

## Business model

Consumers remain free.

Planned revenue layers:

- clearly separated Sponsored offers;
- affiliate and completed-transaction commissions;
- merchant-funded cashback and benefits;
- merchant data and action interfaces;
- developer certification, compute usage, and marketplace participation after distribution exists;
- enterprise administration and private deployment later.

Commercial bids and commission rates must not enter the independent recommendation ranking.

## Cost architecture

The company does not intend to run a fresh large-model workflow over unchanged evidence for every request.

The cost strategy is:

- normalized, source-linked public evidence;
- field-level freshness and change detection;
- deterministic calculation and policy enforcement;
- low-cost model routing;
- strong-model escalation only when needed;
- remote browser execution only as a last method;
- strict per-task search, token, browser-time, retry, and monetary budgets.

The primary economic metric is contribution per 100 completed tasks, including failed and abandoned tasks.

## Current progress

- public GitHub repositories established;
- frontend design and trust model frozen;
- interactive React web prototype implemented;
- homepage, AI intent modes, comparison, sources, Sponsored separation, and full-screen confirmation implemented with demo data;
- responsive, dark-mode, RTL, reduced-motion, and forced-colors support included;
- automated type checking, unit tests, build validation, and static-demo deployment workflow included;
- 14-day U.S. validation and AI cost-control policies documented.

## Next proof points

- public demo URL;
- 20 or more U.S. consumer participants;
- 50 or more completed comparison sessions;
- measured average and p95 task costs;
- Compare-to-merchant-handoff rate;
- evidence that users distinguish independent recommendations from Sponsored content;
- at least one affiliate, merchant, or strategic-commercial route.

## Initial raise rationale

A future pre-seed round would fund a narrow U.S. validation, not an unrestricted global browser launch.

Use of funds would focus on:

- one production-quality shopping vertical;
- evidence acquisition and incremental-update infrastructure;
- cost telemetry and model routing;
- U.S. consumer research;
- security and privacy review;
- affiliate and merchant integrations;
- a limited browser-extension vertical slice.

## Central risk

The central risk is not whether an AI interface can be built. It is whether the platform can provide a trusted AI-native experience at a task cost that advertising, commerce, developer, and enterprise revenue can sustainably cover.

The validation plan is designed to answer that question before scaling engineering spend.
